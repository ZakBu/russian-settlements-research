The frozen R4 selection contains 152,314 rows and 142,361,475 known residents. The official national control is 142,856,536, leaving a measured 495,061 difference. I recomputed current subject totals from R4 and joined them to the official Rosstat regional-control CSV. I folded Nenets into the Arkhangelsk parent and Khanty-Mansi plus Yamal-Nenets into the Tyumen parent, so the 80 disjoint subject controls sum exactly to the national control and the folded R4 subject totals sum exactly to the national selected total. The resulting regional gaps sum to 495,061: 74 controls are below, six equal, and none above. This comparison locates totals only; it does not assign missing people to places.

The largest present control gaps are Moscow Oblast (45,910), Nizhny Novgorod (23,028), Primorye (21,400), Perm (20,287), and Stavropol (14,850). The cached national Table 5 has row overlaps in these subjects, summarized in `top_regional_source_priority.csv`, but those are candidates, not approved replacements. Remaining unique typed rural overlaps nationwide number 1,329. Their Table 5 minus legacy values sum to +141,699; most are not yet publication-bound, and the source-context inventory shows many missing/inherited district labels and some literal district conflicts. The population sum is not an allocation, and the overall Table 5 comparison is not a verified shrink of the regional control gap. Table 5 covers listed cities/urban settlements, rural district centres, and rural localities of at least 3,000; it cannot close small-settlement coverage nationally.

The Dagestan rural 56-row proposal is independently approved but still pending root integration. It carries a measured primary-minus-old delta of +1,549. That mismatch is a reason to preserve the actual row evidence and scope tags, not to clamp values to the control.
