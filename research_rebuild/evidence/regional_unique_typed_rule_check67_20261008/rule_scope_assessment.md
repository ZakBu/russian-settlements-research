## Rule-level assessment

Regional uniqueness is a useful ambiguity screen, not an identity proof by itself. The proposed version adds the right positive gates for current points: a proper physical-place source class, exact current native code, an accepted current physical point, and all same-region/year physical namesakes (including historical rows with NULL county). County can narrow an established homonym; a blank historical county must not make a name unique when another regional namesake exists.

The source-class filter must exclude administrative totals, settlement-group headings, infrastructure/road/station features unless specifically classified as a settlement, and statistical subdivisions that are not independent populated places. Retaining the complete competitor roster makes those checks auditable. Even with a proper place class, multiple same-year source rows for the same locality/administrative scope are a hold until row grain and hierarchy are resolved; a single-row count is not a guarantee of atomic locality grain.

Positive lifecycle evidence overrides stable-name uniqueness. Existing cached example: Сокольники was a city, included within Novомосковск in 2008, and removed from the accounting register. Its old physical settlement row cannot be linked as an ordinary continuing settlement solely because a current point/name is unique. Likewise hold documented post-census creations, dissolved localities, renamed/re-established settlements, and an exact same-year competing populated place. A lack of a known event is not affirmative proof that no event occurred; the existing project method may support retrospective representative-point inference for ordinary stable unique places, but the result should be described as such, not a dated identity fact.

No calibration or false-positive rate is implied. Population evidence is secondary and cannot establish identity or fix a geographic mismatch. Unknown local source coverage, source row grain, or unexamined lifecycle status remains unknown.

## Urban/rural status variation boundaries

A common rural form change does not require a dated legal chronology to infer that a properly named physical locality continues, provided the whole name is unambiguous across all physical-NP types and years in its region/county, a current own-code/own-point anchors the current locality, and no accepted old point/code or positive lifecycle evidence conflicts. Status date stays unknown. The fixed-five Крапивново case exemplifies this rural-to-rural form change.

The larger urban-to-rural examples Субханкулово and Манаскент fail the uniqueness guard as presented. Both have a proper 2002 Table4 urban row and a separate same-name 2002 Table5 rural row. The Table4 row for Субханкулово reads `пгт Субханкулово`, 5,663; a distinct `село Субханкулово`, 386 appears under the printed Нуркеевский сельсовет, while the current 2021 selo is in the Субханкуловский rural municipality. Манаскент has Table4 `пгт Манаскент`, 4,872 and Table5 `село Манаскент`, 4,275 under Карабудахкентский район / Зеленоморский сс. A cached 2009/2011 OKATO/GeoKLADR witness describes a physical village Манаскент, but does not bind the Table4 urban row. The accepted current points for the modern villages cannot choose between the old same-year physical rows. Hold these Table4 mappings unless independently positive old-code/point or lifecycle evidence resolves which old physical object they describe. This is a targeted same-year competitor exception, not a requirement to build a dated dossier for every ordinary rural type change.
