# Target-99 source-grain and spatial-scope audit

**Status:** bounded cached-source audit; no population admission, point/identity admission, source-grain override, or Git action. This reviews whether the federal-city hard exclusions are overconservative for a *spatial representation* goal and what a defensible alternative metric would mean.

## Finding

The present hard block is well-founded for the dataset's current **physical populated-place point** claim, but too broad if read as saying that a federal-city population can never be represented spatially. A city-subject total is a valid observation about its stated administrative territory. It could support a separately named **federal-territory population represented by a point** metric, provided the location means only an explicitly defined representative/label point for that territory. It cannot be silently recoded as population of the physical core settlement, and its point is not a place-boundary or settlement-centroid claim.

For a territory-point metric, the subject total must replace every included child observation in that territory in that year. Do not add the subject total to its named-locality, municipal, district, or rural components. Report the source grain (`physical_settlement`, `federal_city_territory`, or other administrative unit) and point meaning on every row; retain aggregate control status in provenance. Where subject and child partitions cannot be made exhaustive and nonoverlapping, leave the overlap unresolved and exclude it from a single additive coverage numerator. Keep atomic-place and territory-point coverage as separate numerators and labels.

This change of metric could remove a spatial-coverage penalty without solving atomic comparability. Census-date place boundaries and statistical population scope are needed to describe what a count measures; a pre-2012 Moscow boundary is needed only if the requested analysis specifically harmonizes the earlier Moscow footprint with the post-2012 territory. A boundary change by itself does not extinguish the identity of the city. Where authoritative sources explicitly identify the same continuing city, a stable city identity may be recorded across different or incomparable population scopes, while keeping each observation's scope and boundary vintage separate. Unknown boundary comparability must not become a global veto on coordinate identity or ordinary same-place identity.

Identity and observation grain must remain separate. An explicit city-identity link may connect the same named city through time even when one census count measures its physical populated-place core and another measures a larger city territory. The territorial aggregate and the physical populated place are still different entity levels: represent territory/control relationships with a distinct typed link, and do not merge a territory aggregate into an ordinary `same_place` graph that represents atomic populated places. For ordinary identity work, exact historical polygons are not a universal prerequisite; require them only when a particular boundary-equivalence or population-comparability claim depends on them. Do not estimate a core by arbitrary subtraction.

## Source-level exclusions and least-change rule

The six aggregate rows flagged across the three observed years are in the ceiling addendum's exact-key inventory. Current evidence supports these judgments:

- **Moscow 2002:** Table 4 row 2155 (`г. Москва`, 10,126,424) is indented beneath row 2154 (`г. Москва и подчиненные его администрации населенные пункты - городское население`, 10,382,754). This is direct source hierarchy evidence of a distinct city-only child count and remains eligible as a physical-city candidate; it does not establish 2010/2021 comparability. The parent-minus-child difference is not an independently published settlement count.
- **Saint Petersburg 2002:** Table 4 row 3218 gives 4,661,219, equal to the federal-subject control, with district/municipal hierarchy but no separately isolated core. The physical-core exclusion is justified. A territory-point alternative may retain the value under its printed city/subject scope if the metric says so.
- **Moscow and Saint Petersburg 2010:** Table 1.4 directly prints city-labeled values 11,503,501 and 4,879,566, each equal to the respective subject total. It gives no table note that excludes settlements inside the subject territory. Both have `is_federal_aggregate=true` and `federal_aggregate_hard_block` despite `population_scope=settlement`. The old same-place review also blocks the 2002↔2010 Moscow pair as `aggregate_or_nonsettlement_scope`; it blocks the Saint Petersburg pair for both `aggregate_or_nonsettlement_scope` and `aggregate_population_scope`. Therefore the 2010 row tags alone are not enough to release the point-scope guard.
- **2021:** Table 5 roots (Moscow 13,010,112; Saint Petersburg 5,601,911; Sevastopol 547,820) are federal-city controls. The reviewed source supports 32 named urban-place rows for Saint Petersburg and Sevastopol (878,643 total), while holding the 44,399 Kronstadt district-and-city composite. Moscow's 293 secondary locality values total 644,544 but are not individually primary-certified. The 125 Moscow municipal units, 81 Saint Petersburg municipal units, Sevastopol's 449,467 urban-only district subtotal, and Sevastopol's 42,343 rural aggregate do not establish a complete, disjoint physical-core partition. The Sevastopol subtotal is a boundary hypothesis, not a core count.

**Minimal-change proposal:** preserve all six exact source records and their current aggregate flags for atomic-place coverage; add an optional derived metric keyed to a documented `scope_class=federal_city_territory` and a declared representative-point rule. For that metric, apply an exclusive hierarchy: select either a subject total or a complete, source-certified nonoverlapping child partition per subject-year, never both. Keep unresolved territory mass as an aggregate hold. Do not derive a core by subtraction from the 293 Moscow secondary values, infer one from municipal sums, or promote the six aggregates by altering their source grain. Existing named 2021 settlement rows can stay at their reviewed source grain and be counted in the atomic-place measure; the corresponding subject root remains control-only in that measure.

A subject representative point may be an official subject-capital/civic-centre coordinate, but it must be labeled as a territory reference point, not the population-weighted centroid or physical settlement point unless sourced as such. Coordinate source and ID binding still need their own evidence. If the research goal is specifically a map of *settlements*, the territory-point metric is supplementary and cannot satisfy atomic settlement coverage.

## Open evidence needed

To report a census count as a physical populated-place observation, obtain a primary census-date statistical definition of that place and a direct count at that scope or an authoritative, complete crosswalk from census units to it. For 2021 this means the legally or statistically defined city/place territory and how distinct populated places are treated; it does not require reconstructing a pre-2012 Moscow core unless that harmonized footprint is the stated target. For Saint Petersburg, resolve which separately named places/satellites (including Kronstadt) are included in a single-place count. For Sevastopol, determine whether the 449,467 urban-only municipal-district subtotal is a disjoint, exhaustive city/place partition and how Balaklava, Inkerman, and Kacha relate to that definition. For 2010 source scope, obtain an explicit Rosstat note or official census-unit crosswalk explaining whether Table 1.4's city row includes other localities in the subject.

These population-scope requirements do not make a dated polygon mandatory for ordinary city identity. Record explicit source-backed continuity as city identity where available, preserve incomparable census scopes on the observations, and use a separate territory/entity relation for aggregates. Request an exact historical polygon only where boundary equivalence, component partition, or count comparability actually depends on it.
