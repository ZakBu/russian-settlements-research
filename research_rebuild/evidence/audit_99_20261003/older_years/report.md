This is a read-only audit of the current consolidated delivery. It uses the supplied physical missing-point anchors and preserves source IDs/codes as stored. The profile is candidate evidence only; no point or identity was admitted here.

The R5 route counts are coordinate-inventory availability, not accepted point use or proof of same-place identity. In particular, 2010's 2,980 rows with no R5 projection must remain unknown; many have current local source files. The combination of high legacy-route availability and only 13 accepted current carrier paths points to unresolved identity/continuity as the larger measured bottleneck than raw coordinate absence. The 13 graph paths remain subject to the usual population-scope and temporal interpretation limits.

There are 3,133 exact typed historical-point candidates in the physical residual (1,689 for 2002; 1,444 for 2010), carrying 11,210,776 recorded people. These are rows uniquely joined by region, name, and type to raw 2009 classifier and 2011 GeoKLADR objects; the candidate builder itself requires exact name/type, a source additive row, and no known unlocated competitor. The joins establish candidate coordinates, not continuity or population comparability. The audited candidate file treats all points as updated 2011-06-20, not census-date measurements.

The supplied physical anchors exclude three separately recorded nonphysical/city-total rows: 2002 St Petersburg federal-city scope (4,661,219 people), and 2010 Moscow and St Petersburg city-total rows (16,383,067 people). Their exact source IDs and values are in `excluded_nonphysical_rows.csv`.

## Why the historical point candidates remain unaccepted

I joined the current missing-point rows to `extension_application_v2/coordinate_extension_application_checks.parquet`, rather than reusing the older 97k stage counts. The 3,133 candidate rows partition as follows:

| Current v2 result | Rows | Recorded population | Meaning |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Held for 8-digit urban OKATO to GeoKLADR 11-digit bridge not being an exact raw-code join | 1,181 | 9,847,396 | A typed urban code-width bridge candidate; the generic old-year rule still requires `exact_raw_code` |
| Held for within-year shared GeoKLADR point | 1,868 | 654,123 | Several selected rows resolve to one raw point; kept held until the shared-place issue is explained |
| Both code-bridge and shared-point holds | 77 | 708,444 | Both conditions apply |
| Staged pending root review | 7 | 813 | Candidate only; not admitted |

The two hold reason totals overlap: 1,258 rows have the code-bridge hold and 1,945 have the shared-point hold. In the latter group, every candidate point is an exact coordinate collision with another distinct raw historical OKATO object in the same year: 1,340 exact point groups (1,321 two-object groups, 18 three-object groups, one four-object group). These are point-object collisions, not a demonstrated settlement identity conflict. They are not cases where a common administrative code happens to have distinct coordinates: current exact raw OKATO point-candidate groups have one point per code.

The SQL classifier and raw DBF support a scoped typed-city compatibility rule for point association: a literal 8-digit 2009 typed city OKATO can be associated with the corresponding 11-digit GeoKLADR object when the first eight characters agree and raw 2011 `KOD3` is explicitly `000`. For example, the raw Sochi fields are `03426000` and `03426000000`, with `KOD3=000`. This reflects different documented field grains, not lost leading zeros. The existing historical parser encodes that distinction, and the 775-row approved review covers a modern-city cohort only. It does not approve applying the bridge to 2002/2010 observations.

For the old-year typed-city extension, keep the raw codes side by side and validate unique exact physical source name/type, unique historical name/type key, source hashes and locators, raw point plausibility, and same-year point-collision / unlocated-competitor evidence. Treat modern-region concordance as supporting context for a named point, not proof of the historic boundary. Keep PGTs and rural rows out of this typed-city code-width rule until separately reviewed. The current historical-city application has narrower existing conditions when attaching a point to a unique accepted modern carrier: accepted path, independent coordinate origin, a 1 km screen, and event-role review. Those are features of that application, not universal identity requirements. Without a unique modern carrier, the raw historical point remains a coordinate candidate and does not establish cross-year identity. The 1,258 bridge candidates and 1,945 shared-point holds are separate queues; a point-collision review remains necessary even when the code bridge is valid.

For the 3,133 candidate rows, the current application evidence attaches no exact lineage-event IDs. Its dated point source is uniformly GeoKLADR updated 2011-06-20. A separate six-row modern-point retrospective-continuity application remains held for `modern_point_source_exact_code_keyed_event_requires_review` (four 2002 and two 2010 targets, 251,214 people in the earlier v2 candidate check). These are event-sensitive modern-carrier cases, separate from the generic 2011 raw-point candidate cohort. The current accepted graph still has 8 and 5 unique carriers; no physical target in this profile has multiple accepted 2021 carriers.

The separate accepted-final legacy-coordinate hold ledger intersects four current physical residual rows (two per census year). I report these as existing competing-coordinate holds; candidate collisions and missing administrative metadata are not promoted to “actual identity conflicts.” The historical candidate ledger also flags possible unlocated competitors on 2,871 2002 and 10,891 2010 rows. That is a candidate-selection hold, not a demonstrated competing occupied place.

The narrower existing historical-city application overlaps 46 rows from this exact historical candidate set, and all 46 remain held. Its overlapping hold reasons are: no unique accepted modern component carrier (36), candidate/source identity conflict or missing evidence (8), shared historical point for distinct typed objects (9), distance over 1 km to the accepted modern point (4), nonreceiving/unknown event role (7), and unresolved carrier-point conflict (2). These counts overlap. Among the nine candidate rows in the current residual that do have a unique accepted 2021 carrier, two Sochi points coincide with the same GeoKLADR origin used by the carrier, four Wikidata city-point cases carry event or distance holds (Volsk 1.30 km, Baksan 0.81 km, Donskoy 0.13 km, Raichikhinsk 5.23 km), and one rural Venukovo row is 0.15 km from a Tochno 2021 point. This supports the identity bottleneck diagnosis while showing why carrier existence alone is not a point-use rule.

## Identity, source lineage, and soft metadata

The frozen selected older-year rows carry no populated selected `okato` or `oktmo` field in this residual. All residual rows do carry a `source_native_id` value (17,826 distinct values in 2002; 21,707 in 2010), but that field is kept as a literal source-native value and is not asserted to be an official cross-census settlement code. Historical code witnesses from the classifier/GeoKLADR join number 4,363 rows in 2002 and 12,902 in 2010; these are historical raw OKATO-side candidate witnesses, not native census-source official identifiers. The code bridge must never rewrite a census OKTMO or erase leading zeros.

Selected secondary administrative context is materially uneven: `district_raw` is present on 32,845/36,165 physical residual rows in 2002 but only 20,067/72,676 in 2010; `municipality_raw` is present on 29,361 in 2002 and 149 in 2010. Missing district/municipality labels are soft metadata gaps. They do not by themselves disprove a same-place candidate, but they make broad name-only matching unsafe where labels repeat or historic boundaries differ.

Some v2 application-evidence rows lack `source_sha256` and `source_locator` fields, but that is not a release-lineage failure: 3,058 of the 3,133 selected source paths join exactly to the consolidated `input_manifest.parquet`, which supplies their file hashes; the remaining 75 rows carry source hashes and locators in the selected release row. The source record IDs retain workbook/sheet/row or PDF page/row locators. The exact reconciliation is in `selected_source_lineage_reconciliation.csv`. Raw selected source rows were not reopened by this bounded audit. The local raw TOM1 spreadsheets (2002) and Tom11 PDF (2010) are present for many high-mass city rows; the 2010 workbook inventory also confirms regional XLS assets are cached. Historical point lineage is separately pinned by the v4 receipt to the SQL classifier and DBF.

## Useful existing mass rules and source routes

- **Identity/continuity:** use the current accepted same-place graph to stage the 13 unique 2021-carrier rows for the existing continuity review, with census chain and event gates applied. Do not let carrier availability imply a population-boundary claim.
- **Historical raw point:** the next bounded rule review is the 1,258 typed-urban code-bridge candidates, prioritized by recorded population, while retaining the 1,945 shared-point hold. Use the raw classifier and GeoKLADR code blocks to support point association under the scoped schema rule; preserve both literal code strings and do not claim legal identifier equivalence. Require literal name/type/region, source grain, input-manifest file hash and row locator, no coordinate collision, and an explicit interpretation of the 2011 source update. The current seven pending-root rows are a separate, tiny review queue.
- **2010 source row crosswalk:** `population_2010_control_gap_priority_v1` and `primary_rural_remaining_rule_feasibility_v1` establish real cached Table 5 overlap for 1,273 remaining typed rural rows; 178 have the strongest direct district-context subset and 1,037 remain candidate-only under a scoped predicate. This can help bind 2010 publication rows and recover source context. It is not a 2002-to-2010 identity proof or a coordinate source. The 2010 regional XLS files are mostly protected secondary counts, so any value binding must keep their confidentiality limitation and primary-source row review.
- **2002 source recovery:** current `source_native_id`, exact selected source row labels, raw district/municipality strings, and cached TOM1 workbooks provide useful within-source disambiguation material. They still need explicit crosswalk provenance before those values become cross-year identity evidence.

The measured plan should therefore spend its next mass-review stage on identity and coordinate-use rules where an exact row and point witness already exist, while keeping source-lineage and boundary-comparability review visible. Coordinate availability alone cannot carry old census rows across years; the 99% target is not supported by candidate counts.

## Interyear series priorities from existing accepted evidence

The already accepted shared named-point-origin and six scoped region-alias edge batches provide a stronger temporal foundation than the 13 isolated accepted modern-carrier paths: in the existing 2,318-pair 2002–2010 probe, 2,259 endpoint pairs now connect in the frozen same-place graph and 59 remain unlinked. These are identity links only; the source point does not assert that the census populations have matching boundaries, coverage, or measurement dates. The 59 unlinked pairs and 41 event / 19 source-row holds stay separate review queues. The hold-register population is a different cohort from the shared-origin pair file; it includes 1,139 already-applied alias candidates, 41 historical admin-event candidates, and 19 source-row issues. One source-family hold is outside the pair file (IDs are retained in the inventory).

The accepted alias cohort has 1,139 2002–2010 pairs with exact normalized name/type/region context and a single 2021 signature counterpart for 1,138 of them. The new `interyear_2021_continuation_priorities.csv` gives the exact old endpoint IDs, each 2021 counterpart ID, source populations, raw code and point lineage, accepted-point status, and current evidence flags. Of the 1,138 counterparts, 921 already have accepted current points (645 Tochno/DaData and 276 GeoNames); 217 have no accepted point, while one old pair has no unique exact 2021 signature counterpart. None of the 921 point origins is the shared historic GeoKLADR object. Current source-evidence records show no same-year collision or identity-conflict flags for the 1,138 counterpart IDs; the one missing counterpart is naturally unassessed. This is a cheap flag check, not a full graph-collision audit, so it should guide priority rather than be treated as clearance.

Stage these 1,138 exact-signature continuations as identity-review candidates, preserving accepted 2002–2010 edge facts and independently recorded 2021 name/type/region, code, and point context. The 921 with accepted independent modern points are the strongest first tranche, not an automatic same-place decision: different point origin is not by itself a veto, just as a shared origin is not a universal admission rule. Check event roles, same-year competing-place evidence, source grain, and any new graph competitor per candidate. The prior 41 event candidates and 19 source-row candidates remain explicit holds; there are no normalized type changes in that hold register, so changed type does not appear to be the principal blocker there. Current region-boundary changes alone do not break ordinary settlement identity; event and source-row evidence matter.

The small-settlement profile supports a separate row-coverage objective: 775 of the 1,139 old pairs have population at or below 100 in either census, 726 are at or below 100 in both, and 58 include a zero-population record. Track exact record coverage and zero/small-settlement continuity separately from population mass. The current 2021 counterpart populations sum to 360,854 for the 1,138 available rows; they are separate census assertions and are not added to old-year mass or treated as comparable without a source/boundary assessment.

Point recovery and temporal identity remain distinct workstreams. A scoped raw 8-digit/11-digit typed-city code-width bridge could support candidate coordinates for the 2002/2010 rows, but it does not create a 2002–2010 or 2021 same-place link. Likewise, the existing shared point-origin edge batches establish old-year links but do not automatically extend them to 2021. Keep source comparability and boundary harmonization as their own gates after place identity is adjudicated. For a deeper reliable series, first resolve this 2002–2010–2021 candidate chain, then extend backward only through exact primary census publication-row crosswalks and year-specific identity/event review; retain zero- and small-population localities in the same queue so mass coverage does not mask record loss.
