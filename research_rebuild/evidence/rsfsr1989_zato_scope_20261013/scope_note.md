# Closed-city overlap in published 1989 urban counts

Conclusion: independently recovered 1989 ZATO populations are non-additive with the original published 1989 urban city totals. Absence of a named closed-city row does not imply its residents were excluded. Published city/territorial totals included redistributed closed-city residents. Equality between the 3,229-row primary sum (108,425,580, supplied by parent audit) and national present-urban control is consistent with this accounting. This note adds no population observations and changes no integration files.

The preceding paragraph says Snezhinsk and Tryokhgorny were counted outside Chelyabinsk oblast, in other oblasts. ## Seversk/Tomsk and Sarov/Gorky

A.E. Raisikh, *Парадоксы учета населения закрытых городов в СССР и последующая городская динамика*, Izvestiya RAN Geography, 2024, 88(5), 770–786, DOI 10.31857/S2587556624050111. Accessible full article: https://journals.rcsi.science/2587-5566/article/view/289890 . Locators: section “МЕТОДИКА ОЦЕНКИ НАСЕЛЕНИЯ ЗАТО В 1989 ГОДУ”, Tables 1–2; footnotes 10–16; paragraph discussing the initial atomic cities; final conclusions.

Author reconstruction: Seversk was distributed between Tomsk oblast and Kemerovo oblast; Sarov between Gorky/Nizhny Novgorod and Kirov oblasts. Table 2 shows Tomsk 502 thousand in original census publication versus 473 thousand in the 2003 publication; 3 thousand annexed settlement adjustment yields a reconstructed 32 thousand ZATO attribution. Seversk itself has no original open-publication count but 110 thousand in the later source. These are rounded author comparisons, not an exact additive/subtractive crosswalk. Tomsk is NOT the sole acceptor for Seversk.

The official source underlying revised permanent counts is *Российский статистический ежегодник, 2003*, Goskomstat Russia, pp. 75–79 (regional counts) and 79–81 (cities); Seversk p. 81. The author compares the old published counts with corrected permanent counts, after annexation changes. This note has not downloaded the original 2003 yearbook and does not elevate the inferred Seversk/Kemerovo or Sarov/Kirov mapping to a directly stated official rule. The acquired Chelyabinskstat statement is direct official proof of the redistribution practice.

## What the original sources say

Demoscope https://www.demoscope.ru/weekly/ssp/rus89_reg2.php footer cites the 1989 census, Tom 1, Part 1, Table 3, explicitly “Численность наличного населения”. It does not supply a ZATO exclusion note. Tomsk original city row is 501,963 (present population).

Goskomstat RSFSR, *РСФСР. Городские поселения*, 1991, local `/workspace/shared/urban1989_scan_inventory.pdf`, preface on scan/PDF page 3: city and urban-settlement counts are supplied for both present and permanent population; groupings and demographic characteristics use permanent. The city table excludes subordinate settlements of the same-named city council. That administrative-scope statement does not claim the removal of secrecy-related attributed closed-city residents. First five scans were checked; no closed-city exclusion statement was found in that bounded section.

## Practical consequence

Distinct settlement/root UIDs do not guarantee distinct people. Retaining an inflated original open-city count and adding an independently recovered closed-city count double counts the attributed residents. Until an exact same-basis redistribution crosswalk and corrected acceptor counts are available, retain those ZATO observations as linked source evidence with `non_additive_scope_overlap` (or exclude them from the additive coverage subtotal). Do not automatically subtract the rounded 2024 author reconstruction from Demoscope present counts, because the reconstruction uses permanent counts and territorial adjustments. No overlap deduction was computed here.
